Oak, walnut, or painted cabinetry?

An honest guide to the three cabinetry families — how they age, what they cost, and which rooms they love.

Bilal Khan

Joinery Workshop Lead

Aug 2, 2026 · 4 min read

Cabinetry is the biggest surface in most rooms, so the wood decision outweighs almost every other. Here is how we guide clients at the sample table.

Oak — the worker

White-oiled oak takes daily life, hides small marks in its grain, and moves gracefully between modern and classic rooms. It is our default for family kitchens and high-traffic joinery.

Walnut — the statement

Darker, richer, and happiest in rooms with good light and calmer palettes. We reserve it for libraries, headboards and statement pieces where it can be the hero rather than the background.

Painted — the chameleon

Painted MDF and birch plywood suits walls of integrated joinery: it disappears into the architecture and can be re-toned in ten years without replacing a door. Check that edges are solid-lipped — that is where cheap painted cabinetry shows its age first.

  • Kitchens and kids' rooms: oak or painted
  • Studies and bedrooms: walnut where budget allows
  • Whole-wall storage: painted for a calm envelope
  • Hardware: brass ages best, matte black photographs best
