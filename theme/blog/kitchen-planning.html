How to plan a kitchen that works every day

The five zones that decide whether your kitchen is a joy or a bottleneck — and how to test them before you build.

Sana Malik

Design Director

Aug 18, 2026 · 6 min read

Most kitchens fail long before the style question. They fail at 7am with three people trying to make breakfast, or on Eid when four dishes need the same counter. Planning is the design — here is how we test a kitchen before a single cabinet is drawn.

The five zones

Every kitchen we draw is set out as five zones: consumables, non-consumables, cleaning, preparation and cooking. The distances between them — not the finishes — decide how the room feels to use.

  • Consumables near the fridge and one step from the prep counter
  • Plates and glasses between the dishwasher and the table
  • A prep run of at least 90cm of clear, lit worktop
  • Cooking zone with landing space on both sides of the hob
  • A cleaning zone that never blocks the cook when the sink is in use
If two people cannot pass behind an open dishwasher and oven door, the kitchen is too tight — whatever it looks like.

The newspaper test

Lay out your current kitchen's problems on paper: where do shopping bags land, where does the toast queue form, which drawer is always half-open? A new kitchen that does not answer those three habits is just new cupboards.

When we plan kitchens at Woodex, every drawer is specified to its contents — spices, pots, lunchboxes — before joinery drawings are issued. It is the least glamorous part of design and the part clients thank us for years later.
