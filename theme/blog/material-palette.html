How to build a material palette that ages well

The 60-30-10 of materials, why we sample in the room's own light, and the patina test.

Ahmed Raza

Founder & Lead Designer

May 20, 2026 · 4 min read

A material palette is a promise about the future: how the room will look in year ten, not on handover day. This is our process for keeping that promise.

Start with the surfaces you cannot change

Structure, stone, big windows — build the palette outward from what is fixed. The floor and the largest joinery run should be decided together, in the same sample session, in the room's actual light.

60-30-10, the honest version

Sixty percent neutral envelope, thirty percent supporting texture, ten percent accent that you can afford to fall out of love with. Kitchens: painted envelope, oak run, brass hardware. Bedrooms: linen envelope, oak headboard, one colour.

The patina test

Ask of every material: where will this be touched most, and what will it look like after ten years of that touch? Brass, oiled oak and lime plaster improve. High-gloss lacquer and dark stone show everything. Choose your battles.

We keep a library of over four hundred samples at the studio — clients touch, tilt and daylight-test everything before a single order is placed.
