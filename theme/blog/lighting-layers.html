Three layers of light — the difference rooms are remembered by

Ambient, task and accent: how to layer light so a room flatters people, food and evenings.

Ahmed Raza

Founder & Lead Designer

Jul 5, 2026 · 5 min read

One ceiling light makes a room flat and everyone in it tired. Every memorable interior we have delivered uses the same three-layer logic — and it costs less than people expect to get right.

Layer one: ambient

The even, gentle base — cove lighting, shaded pendants, wall washes. Warm white (2700–3000K), always dimmable. This is the layer that sets mood and should never come from a single central fitting.

Layer two: task

Light where work happens: under-cabinet strips for counters, a focused beam over the reading chair, mirror lights at face height in bathrooms. Position task light between your eye and the task — never behind you.

Layer three: accent

The layer that flatters: picture lights, an uplit plant, a low pendant over the dining table at 60–70cm above the surface. Accent light is what makes guests ask why the room feels so good.

  • Every room: at least two circuits, ideally three
  • Dining pendant: 60–70cm above the table
  • Bedside: warm light you can switch from the pillow
  • Avoid: bright downlights directly over sofas and beds
